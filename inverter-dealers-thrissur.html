Understanding your inverter requirements

A suitable backup system is selected by understanding what you want to keep running during a power cut and for how long.

Homes and workplaces may need backup for different combinations of lights, fans, routers, computers, refrigerators or other equipment. Make a list of the appliances that must remain operational. Note their power ratings where available, whether they start together, and how long you expect to use them during an outage.

The total running load helps determine inverter capacity, while the required backup duration influences battery capacity. A system should also account for surge requirements, battery voltage compatibility, charging conditions and the quality of the electrical installation.

Useful starting point: Share your appliance list and expected backup time when requesting advice. A product recommendation without this information may not match your actual requirement.

Battery chemistry, capacity, voltage, charging compatibility and maintenance requirements all affect how a backup system performs.

LithiumA rechargeable battery technology available in different chemistries and system configurations.Confirm inverter compatibility, battery management system requirements, operating limits, protection and warranty terms.

Understand Ah and voltage

Ampere-hour (Ah) is a measure of electric charge capacity at a stated rating condition. Battery voltage must match the inverter's supported battery-bank voltage. Do not compare Ah figures alone when batteries have different voltages or chemistries.

Charging, lifespan and warranty

Charging time depends on battery capacity, depth of discharge, charger output, battery condition and the charging profile. Battery life varies with temperature, usage, maintenance and charging practices. Read the manufacturer's warranty exclusions and service terms.

Maintenance reminder: Follow the battery manufacturer's instructions. For serviceable lead-acid batteries, check electrolyte levels only as directed; keep terminals clean and the area ventilated. Never open or service a sealed battery.

Safe and reliable operation

Inverter installation and maintenance

Correct installation helps the system operate within its design limits and reduces avoidable electrical and battery-related risks.

Choose a suitable location

Install equipment in a dry, stable and appropriately ventilated location, following the manufacturer's clearance and temperature guidance.

Use correct wiring and protection

Cable sizing, fuses or breakers, isolation and connections should be selected for the system rating and applicable electrical requirements.

Check earthing and polarity

Proper earthing, polarity and protective arrangements are important. Installation and electrical testing should be handled by a qualified professional.

Maintain battery ventilation

Follow battery-specific ventilation and placement requirements. Keep ignition sources away from batteries that may release gases during charging.

Inspect periodically

Look for unusual heat, damaged cables, corrosion, warning indicators or changes in backup performance, and arrange inspection when needed.

Follow manufacturer guidance

Use the recommended charging profile and maintenance schedule. Avoid modifying electrical connections or opening equipment yourself.

Solar energy solutions

Solar inverters and hybrid power systems

Solar systems can be configured in different ways depending on grid availability, backup needs and the role of battery storage.

On-grid solar

An on-grid system operates in coordination with the electricity grid. Standard grid-tied systems generally shut down during a grid outage for safety unless designed with a separate backup function.

Off-grid solar

An off-grid system is designed to operate independently of the utility grid and typically relies on battery storage to supply energy when solar generation is insufficient.

Hybrid solar

A hybrid system combines solar generation with grid interaction and battery storage, subject to the inverter's design and configuration. It can be considered when both solar use and backup capability are important.

Regular inverter vs solar inverter

A conventional inverter generally converts stored battery DC power into AC power for connected loads. A solar inverter is designed to work with solar panel generation; hybrid models may also manage battery storage and grid supply.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Answers to common questions about choosing and using inverter backup systems.

How do I choose an inverter for my home?

List the appliances you want to run, add their running loads, identify any starting surge requirements and decide how long you need backup. Then select an inverter with suitable capacity and match it with a compatible battery bank. A qualified professional can help check the final configuration.

Which inverter is suitable for a 2BHK house?

The number of bedrooms alone is not enough to determine inverter size. A 2BHK home with a few lights and fans has a different requirement from one that also needs to run a refrigerator, computer or other high-load appliances. Prepare an appliance list and backup-time requirement before selecting a system.

What is the difference between a UPS and an inverter?

Both can provide backup power, but they may differ in transfer time, output conditioning, design and intended application. Some UPS systems are designed for sensitive equipment and tighter power continuity requirements. Compare the specifications and use case rather than relying only on the product name.

Which battery is better, tubular or lithium?

Neither is universally better. Tubular lead-acid batteries are a common inverter option and have specific maintenance needs. Lithium batteries can offer different operating characteristics but require compatible charging, battery management and protection. Compare total system cost, compatibility, warranty and usage pattern.

How long can a 150Ah battery provide backup?

There is no single runtime for a 150Ah battery. Runtime depends on battery voltage, chemistry, condition, usable depth of discharge, inverter efficiency and the connected load. A 150Ah rating by itself is not enough to calculate a reliable backup duration.

Can I connect solar panels to an inverter?

Only when the inverter is designed to accept solar input or is paired with a compatible solar charge controller and system arrangement. Check the inverter's supported PV voltage, current, power limits and battery charging requirements before connecting panels.

What is the difference between on-grid and hybrid inverters?

An on-grid inverter works with the utility grid and a standard grid-tied system normally stops exporting or supplying power during an outage for safety. A hybrid inverter can coordinate solar, grid and battery functions when designed and configured for backup operation.

How much does inverter installation cost?

Installation cost varies with the inverter and battery configuration, wiring distance, mounting or placement needs, protection equipment, changeover arrangements and site conditions. Request a quotation that clearly lists equipment, materials, labour and exclusions.

How often should an inverter battery be maintained?

Follow the battery manufacturer's maintenance schedule. Serviceable lead-acid batteries may require periodic checks, while sealed batteries and lithium systems have different requirements. Keep the installation area clean and ventilated, and arrange professional inspection if you notice swelling, leakage, unusual heat or reduced performance.
